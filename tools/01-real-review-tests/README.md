# Three tests for a real review

**From:** The 5am Diary, entry 1, "What 'a human decides' has to mean" (1 Dec 2026)
**Time:** about an hour a month · **Needs:** your case system's export or a sample of closed alerts · **No AI needed**

Everyone says "a human decides". The costliest fines in recent years came from reviews that looked like checking and weren't. These three tests show whether yours is real.

## The tests

### 1. Can the reviewer see what raised the alert?
Open ten recently closed alerts as the reviewer saw them.

- [ ] The triggering transaction, match or document is on the screen, not just a score.
- [ ] The reviewer can say in one sentence why it fired.

If they see a score but not its cause, they can't disagree with it.

### 2. How often do reviewers overrule the system?
From last month's closed alerts, count:

| | Count |
|---|---|
| Alerts closed in line with the system's suggestion | |
| Alerts where the reviewer disagreed | |
| Alerts closed with a vague reason ("other", "undecided", blank) | |

An overrule rate near zero, or many vague reasons, is a warning sign. Measure challenge, not speed.

### 3. Would you catch a case you planted?
Each month, with your MLRO's agreement, seed three to five known bad test cases into the queue.

- [ ] Record how many come back flagged and correctly closed.
- [ ] Record how long each took.

## What this can't do
It won't prove your rules are right, only whether people are really reviewing what the rules produce. Agree test cases with your MLRO before seeding them, so they never reach a real report.
